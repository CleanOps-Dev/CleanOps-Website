Clean Ops T7 - Privacy Policy

Last updated: 18 October 2026

This policy explains which personal data Clean Ops T7 ("Clean Ops", "the Patch", "we") processes, why, for how long, who receives it, and which rights you have under the EU General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR). It covers the Patch (the d3d11.dll in your Black Ops III folder), the Clean Ops server it connects to, our Discord server, and the website cleanops.dev.

It also covers people who don't use Clean Ops: players who are reported by Clean Ops users or put on our blacklist (sections 3.3 and 3.4).

1. Who is responsible (controller)

Krashgamer, on behalf of the Clean Ops project

E-mail: privacy@cleanops.dev

We are a small, non-commercial community project. We have not appointed a data protection officer (we are not required to, Art. 37 GDPR).

2. Summary

  • The Patch connects to our server every time you play. The server learns your Steam ID, a pseudonymised form of your IP address (your real IP isn't stored), the Patch version and when you were online.
  • Steam confirms that the Steam ID is really yours.
  • Connection and security records are deleted after 7 days (backups included).
  • Our blacklist (players confirmed as cheaters) and watchlist are kept until a moderator removes the entry.
  • Cheat reports you send contain your Steam ID and the suspect's Steam ID, in-game names and scoreboard values. Our moderators review them on Discord. Reports are kept permanently as the documentation behind our bans.
  • If you host a P2P match, its address is shared with Clean Ops players so their game can recognise it as a genuine Clean Ops session.
  • We don't sell data, show ads or use tracking/analytics.

3. What we process, why, and on which legal basis

3.1 Connecting to the Clean Ops server (every game launch)

What: your Steam ID64; your IP address, which is replaced by a pseudonym before it reaches our application (see below); the Patch version; technical connection identifiers; connect and disconnect times; and connection activity while you are connected.

IP pseudonymisation: our server replaces your IP address with a keyed hash before the request reaches the application. Your real IP is never written to disk or logged by us. The hash is still personal data: it lets us recognise the same connection again, which is its purpose.

Why: to deliver the blacklist and settings to your game, to keep the connection stable, and to protect the service from abuse.

Legal basis: Art. 6(1)(b) GDPR - necessary to provide the Patch you chose to use; and Art. 6(1)(f) - our legitimate interest in a secure, working service.

Is it required? Yes. The Patch needs this connection to work; without it, Clean Ops and online play with it may not be available.

3.2 Steam identity check

What: when connecting, the Patch obtains an authentication ticket from your Steam client and sends it to our server, which has it checked by Valve's Steam Web API. Steam answers with the Steam ID the ticket belongs to and, under Steam Family Sharing, the account that owns the game licence.

Why / legal basis: Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR - making sure nobody can pretend to be another player, and preventing ban evasion.

3.3 Anti-cheat: blacklist, watchlist and ban-evasion detection

Blacklist. Players whom our moderators have confirmed as cheating are put on the blacklist. Stored: Steam ID, the name known at the time, the reason, the date, and which moderator added the entry. Every Clean Ops user's game receives the blacklisted Steam IDs (no names, no reasons) and removes those players from matches automatically. A blacklisted player can't use Clean Ops. When someone is newly blacklisted, connected Clean Ops users see a short "Player Banned" notice with the Steam ID.

Watchlist. Accounts under observation by our moderators. Same data as the blacklist; it has no effect on play.

Ban-evasion detection. We keep a record of which Steam IDs connected from which pseudonymised IP addresses within the last 7 days, and of Family Sharing links, to detect banned players returning on other accounts. Possible links are shown to moderators as a hint; they never block anyone by themselves - a moderator decides.

Why: to keep cheaters out of matches where Clean Ops users play and to stop banned players from coming back on other accounts - the core purpose of Clean Ops.

Legal basis: Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR - our legitimate interest, and that of every other player, in fair, cheat-free matches. We have documented the balance of interests in a Legitimate Interests Assessment.

This also applies to players who don't use Clean Ops: a cheater can be blacklisted based on reports by Clean Ops users (section 3.4). Their data then comes from those reports (Art. 14 GDPR); the source is the reporting player's game.

3.4 Cheat reports

What: when you report a player in-game, the report contains: the suspect's Steam ID and in-game name, their scoreboard values (e.g. kills, deaths, ping, team), the reason you chose, your in-game name, and match information (server, game mode, match time). Your Steam ID is added by the server from your connection. We also store information that helps moderators judge the report, such as a reliability score of the reporter, and count reports for rate limits.

Reports are posted to a private moderation channel on our Discord server, where moderators review them. Reporters who repeatedly send unfounded reports can be warned or excluded from reporting.

Why: to identify cheaters and to tell genuine reports from fake or abusive ones.

Why we keep reports permanently: reports are the documentation behind our blacklist decisions and appeals.

Legal basis: Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR - fair play and protecting the report system from abuse.

3.5 P2P hosting (only if you host)

What: in Black Ops III, players joining a P2P match connect to the host's IP address - that is how P2P works in the game. When you host with Clean Ops, your game also sends this public IP address to our server, which keeps it in memory only (never on disk) while you host and shares it - without your Steam ID - with connected Clean Ops users. Moderators are notified with your Steam ID when you start or stop hosting.

Why: so that Clean Ops can tell genuine Clean Ops P2P sessions (and official servers) apart from unknown ones and keep malicious sessions away from its users. When you stop hosting, the address is removed from the list.

Legal basis: Art. 6(1)(b) GDPR - the hosting feature you chose to use; Art. 6(1)(f) - protecting players from malicious sessions.

3.6 Security and abuse protection

What: records of refused connections and of suspected abuse or tampering, with the Steam ID and the data involved; moderator actions (who changed which list or setting); the server's technical logs (which may contain Steam IDs).

Legal basis: Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR - security of the service (see also Art. 32 GDPR).

3.7 Processing that happens only on your PC, and direct requests to third parties

The Patch processes the following on your computer and does not send it to us:

  • Data the game already gives it about other players in your match (names, Steam IDs, IP addresses), to find and remove blacklisted players and to block known attacks.
  • A local log file in your game folder under cleanops\logs\ (events shown in the Clean Ops console, which may include other players' names and Steam IDs) and your settings in config.json. You can delete these files at any time.
  • Discord Rich Presence (on by default, can be switched off in the Clean Ops settings): your local Discord app shows your current game mode, map and lobby size to your Discord contacts, under Discord's terms.

Two requests go from your PC to third parties directly:

  • Location lookup - ip-api.com (operated by Artia Soft, Romania, EU): at game start the Patch asks ip-api.com for the approximate location of your IP address, to pick the nearest region for P2P hosting. ip-api.com receives your IP address. Only the chosen region is used; the result isn't sent to us. Legal basis: Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR.
  • Update check - GitHub (GitHub Inc. / Microsoft, USA): at game start the Patch checks GitHub for a newer version and downloads it from there if available. GitHub receives your IP address, as with any download. Legal basis: Art. 6(1)(b) and (f) GDPR (keeping the Patch working and secure).

3.8 Our Discord server

If you join our Discord server, Discord Inc. processes your data under Discord's own privacy policy. In addition, we:

  • review cheat reports and moderation notices there (3.3–3.6);
  • handle support requests and ban appeals in tickets;
  • log messages posted, edited and deleted in our support channels to a private moderator channel, so that moderation decisions can be traced and abuse (e.g. deleted threats) stays visible.

Legal basis: Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR - running and moderating the community; Art. 6(1)(b) for answering your requests.

3.9 The website (cleanops.dev)

The website is hosted on GitHub Pages (GitHub Inc., USA). GitHub processes your IP address and technical request data to deliver the page and for security (see GitHub's privacy statement). The page also loads the FAQ and the current version number from GitHub. Fonts are part of the site itself. The site sets no cookies of its own and uses no analytics or tracking.

The installation guide video is embedded from YouTube (Google Ireland Ltd. / Google LLC, USA). When the page loads, your browser connects to YouTube, which receives your IP address and technical data and may set cookies under Google's privacy policy.

Legal basis: Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR - presenting the project and showing how to install it.

5. Transfers outside the EU/EEA

Cloudflare, Valve, Discord, GitHub and Google are based in, or transfer data to, the USA. All five are certified under the EU–U.S. Data Privacy Framework, for which the European Commission has issued an adequacy decision (Art. 45 GDPR). Cloudflare and Oracle additionally include the EU Standard Contractual Clauses (Art. 46 GDPR) in their data processing agreements. Our server and database are in Germany.

7. Automated decisions and profiling

No decision about you is made solely by a machine (Art. 22 GDPR). Blacklisting is always decided by a moderator after reviewing reports. Once you are blacklisted, the removal from matches is carried out automatically by the Patch on every Clean Ops user's PC.

The reliability score of reports (3.4) and the ban-evasion hints (3.3) are automated evaluations ("profiling"). They are shown to moderators as an aid and never lead to a decision on their own.

8. Your rights

You have the right to access (Art. 15), rectification (Art. 16), erasure (Art. 17), restriction of processing (Art. 18), data portability (Art. 20) and to lodge a complaint with a data protection supervisory authority (Art. 77), for example the one in the EU country where you live.

Right to object (Art. 21 GDPR): where we rely on legitimate interests (Art. 6(1)(f)), you can object at any time on grounds relating to your particular situation. We then stop, unless we have compelling legitimate grounds that override your interests - for example keeping a confirmed cheater on the blacklist. The same applies to erasure (Art. 17(1)(c)). We look at every request individually.

To use your rights, write to privacy@cleanops.dev with your Steam ID64. We need to confirm that the Steam account is yours before we disclose or delete anything (for example with a Steam message from that account). We answer within one month.

If you think you were blacklisted by mistake, open a ban appeal ticket on our Discord server (Steam profile link or Steam ID64, and what happened). Appeals are reviewed by a moderator. You can also e-mail us.

9. Children

Clean Ops is not intended for children under 16. Call of Duty: Black Ops III itself is rated 18 (PEGI / USK). If you believe a child under 16 has given us personal data, contact us and we will delete it.

10. Security

Connections are encrypted and protected against tampering; IP addresses are pseudonymised before they reach our application; our systems are not reachable from the internet beyond what the Patch needs; access is limited to the people who need it. More detail: our Privacy by Design & Default statement.

If a data breach is likely to result in a high risk for you, we will inform you through our Discord server and website and notify the supervisory authority as required by Art. 33–34 GDPR.

11. Changes

We update this policy when our processing changes. The current version is always available on cleanops.dev; the date at the top shows when it last changed.
