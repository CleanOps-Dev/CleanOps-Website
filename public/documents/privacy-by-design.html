Clean Ops T7 - Data Protection by Design & Default

Last updated: 18 October 2026

Article 25 GDPR asks us to build data protection into how Clean Ops works, not add it later. This page summarises how we do that. What data we process and why is in our Privacy Policy.

Collect little

  • The server learns your Steam ID, a pseudonymised IP address, the Patch version and when you play. No e-mail, no real name, no hardware IDs, no payment data.
  • Cheat reports are checked on the server and only the fields we need are kept.
  • Game clients receive only what they need: the IDs on the blacklist - no names, no reasons.
  • P2P host addresses are kept in memory only, never on disk, and are shared without Steam IDs.

Your IP address is pseudonymised

  • For the connection to our server, your IP address is replaced with a keyed hash before it reaches our application. Our server keeps no access logs with IP addresses.
  • The only exception is P2P hosting, where the host's address is needed for the session itself; it is kept in memory only, never on disk (Privacy Policy, section 3.5).

Keep it short

  • Connection, security and ban-evasion records are deleted automatically after 7 days - including in our backups, our logs and our Discord notification channel.

People decide, not machines

  • Automated hints are shown to moderators as hints; they never ban anyone by themselves. Every ban is a moderator's decision and can be appealed.

Secure by default

  • Connections are encrypted and protected against tampering.
  • Our database and admin tools are not reachable from the internet.
  • Abuse limits protect the service and everyone's data.

Privacy-friendly defaults in the Patch

  • IP spoofing is on by default, to hide your IP address from other players where the game would expose it.
  • Discord Rich Presence can be switched off in the settings.

Review

We review these measures whenever the way Clean Ops processes data changes, and at least once a year.
